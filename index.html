FOUNDATIONS OF MACROECONOMICS — THE AGGREGATE SYSTEM

Macroeconomic Environment & The Four Core Markets

While microeconomics analyzes the pricing and output decisions of an individual firm, macroeconomics investigates the aggregate behavior of the whole economy — output, inflation, employment, interest rates, and balance of payments.

1. Goods Market

Equilibrium of aggregate output ($Y$) and total expenditure ($C + I + G + NX$). Determines national output and product price level.

Key variable: Output ($Y$)

2. Money Market

Interaction between real money supply ($M/P$) controlled by Central Bank and real money demand ($L$). Clears via interest rate.

Key variable: Interest rate ($i$)

3. Bond Market

Long-term debt instruments and sovereign government paper. Prices inversely mirror yields: when bond prices rise, interest rates fall.

Key variable: Yield / Price ($P_B$)

4. Labour Market

Firms demand labor up to marginal revenue product; households supply labor. Defines employment level ($N$) and the real wage rate.

Key variable: Real Wage ($W/P$)

Circular Flow: Leakages vs. Injections Equilibrium

Total Leakages ($S + T + M$)

Income withdrawn from the domestic expenditure stream: Private Savings ($S$), Net Taxes ($T$), and Foreign Imports ($M$).

Total Injections ($I + G + X$)

Exogenous demand injections into domestic production: Planned Investment ($I$), Government Purchases ($G$), and Foreign Exports ($X$).
