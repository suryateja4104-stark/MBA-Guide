 The Phillips Curve
•   1958 A. W. Phillips, Plotted the United Kingdom for the years
1861– 1957
 •   The Phillips curve shows that the rate of wage inflation decreases
with the unemployment rate.
 • The Phillips curve is an inverse relationship between the rate of
unemployment and the rate of increase in money wages.
 •   The higher the rate of unemployment, the lower the rate of wage
inflation.
 • In other words, there is a tradeoff between wage inflation and
unemployment.

 Height of the short-run Phillips curve
•   Instead of intersecting the natural rate of unemployment at
zero, the modern Phillips curve intersects the natural rate at the
level of expected inflation.
 • Firms and workers adjust their expectations of inflation in
light of the recent history of inflation.
 •   The Curves show the same short-run tradeoff between
unemployment and inflation – slopes are equal
 •   Full employment was compatible with roughly 2 percent
annual inflation; in the early 1980s full employment was
compatible with roughly 7 percent inflation.
